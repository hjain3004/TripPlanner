# Travel artwork house standard

Creative brief: calm, premium, destination-specific travel artwork inside a neo-brutalist postcard system. The user's named shades were examples of mood; they are not a required palette. No global palette or destination pack has been approved by this workflow.

## Constant craft, variable place

Keep postcard geometry, clear hierarchy, functional typography, strong rules and deliberate hard shadows consistent. Express the destination through subject, materials, photographic/illustrative treatment and a researched palette. Develop country packs first; city or seasonal variants need a distinct brief. A multi-stop trip can keep a quiet shared canvas with local artwork per stop.

Derive colors from several credible references, not flag colors or stereotypes. Compare directions across a hero, postcard and dense planning panel. Use muted large surfaces with enough dark/light separation for controls and text. Exact UI colors are applied by semantic tokens/templates; a generation prompt cannot guarantee exact hex values in pixels.

## Artwork families

- Observational editorial imagery: believable viewpoints, natural light, plausible materials and restrained grading. For factual hotel/venue depictions use reviewed real imagery; generated scenes are illustrative art, not inventory evidence.
- Print illustration: intentional shapes, coherent line work, a chosen print process and controlled ink variation. Avoid treating all destinations as the same vintage poster with a different landmark.
- Detail studies: a specific architectural, landscape or material detail with clear composition. These provide visual variety without generic travel collages.

Choose the family per brief. Photorealism is an option, not a requirement for every asset. Polish does not mean hyper-sharpening, uniform gloss, HDR halos or artificial surface detail. Describe the intended materials/light/composition instead of relying on "premium, masterpiece, 8K."

## Composition and assembly

Generate the artwork separately. Add postcard frames, titles, dates and captions through reviewed SVG/HTML/CSS templates. Keep financial outcomes and trip-specific fields in accessible UI text copied from typed backend artifacts. Never bake amounts, availability, recommendations or verification into generated images.

Specify subject position, focal point and safe crop area for each placement. Make a new composition when portrait and landscape crops cannot both preserve the subject. Rotation and texture belong to editorial artwork; questions, controls and financial ledgers stay aligned and legible.

Existing repository philatelic stamps are restricted to explicit Japan editorial placements. This workflow does not relax that allowlist. New destination postcard artwork can be prepared here, but product integration is separate work. Stamps never carry trust, payment, booking or transfer meaning.

## Quality and provenance

Judge geometry, destination fidelity, texture, lighting, visual hierarchy and actual display-size crops. Check originals at 100% and in mobile/desktop compositions. Generated art may be visually excellent; do not promise universal indistinguishability or claim human/photographic authorship.

Record tool, observed model, exact prompt, reference uses, original file hash and reviewer selection. Use a native clean-export setting when available and permitted; retain invisible SynthID and origin records. If clean export is unavailable, report the limitation rather than concealing the mark or changing account region. Reference rights remain attached to the reference; unknown rights are not silently cleared by generation.

Use existing Google AI Pro access with USD 0 additional spend. Work within available included quota/credits. Pause at exhaustion; do not buy credits, upgrade, enable API billing or switch to a paid fallback. Create assets offline before release; the application never generates artwork at request time.
